# Escrow

**1.**
An idea kept in a coat pocket all winter, folded twice,
soft at the crease where a thumb worries it.
By the tenth reading there's a hole worn through the middle
and I can see the street through it, gray and moving.

**2.**
The shortest month, and I spend it filling a form:
one field for what, one for how much, one for when.
The when is a guess. The how much is a number
that stops my hand above the button like cold water.

**3.**
Somebody three timezones off reads it at 2 a.m.,
in a thaw, with the radiator ticking,
and thinks: I could do that. I have the thing. I have the wall.
They don't know me. The bounty doesn't care who's awake.

**4.**
The fool's month, and the oldest sentence in the world
is not *I love you*. It's *prove it*. Show me the crack
in the wall where you say the crack is, and the wall behind it.
Pics or it didn't happen — six words, no appeal.

**5.**
So they take the picture. Bad light, thumb in the corner,
the thing itself small in the middle of the frame
like a fact that would rather not be photographed.
It's enough. May light, and it's exactly enough.

**6.**
Then the doubting, which is healthy, which is the point:
check the shadow, check the second hand, check the timestamp,
is that wall where they say the wall is. Long light, no sleep.
Doubt is the audit. The claim survives it or it doesn't.

**7.**
And all this while the money sits in escrow, patient —
not in a pocket, not earning, not mine yet,
a number held by arithmetic and other people's machines
the way a held breath is held by a body.

**8.**
In August a man photographs a sandwich at arm's length
to prove he ate it, and the chain of custody
is his own thumb, moist, in the lower left,
and the issuer studies this and writes: approved.

**9.**
September. The acceptance is one gray button and a wait.
Gas comes out first, before a cent moves —
the network's small toll on sincerity. An address I've never seen
receives what I said I'd give. It lands. Nobody claps.

**10.**
October now, and it stays: the claim, the photo, the gray yes,
public and permanent, nobody's to take back.
On the leaderboard my name is a line of numbers
that will outlast the sandwich, the wall, and the doubt.

---

**Human edit note**

Stanzas were drafted with AI assistance from my own outline (pocket-and-form opening, one sandwich joke, payout with no applause), then rewritten line by line by hand: I cut two forced rhymes, replaced "in the space between" with the coat pocket, rewrote stanza 6 entirely for scansion, and changed the closing image from "proof of me" to the leaderboard numbers so it lands on the platform instead of on feeling.
Every line here was read aloud and adjusted for breath; no stanza is pasted raw from a model.
The sandwich stanza is mine, and I will not be taking questions about it.
